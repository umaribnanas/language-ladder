# Contributing to Language Ladder

Thank you for helping make language learning more useful, accurate, and welcoming. Contributions can be large or small: a corrected phrase, a vocabulary entry, a lesson, a cultural note, or an improvement to the repository structure.

Language Ladder is actively looking for maintainers and long-term contributors.
The project should be able to continue growing if the original author becomes
busy or eventually steps away. Shared stewardship is welcome and encouraged.

## Before you contribute

- Read the [README](README.md) to understand the curriculum and comparison-table approach.
- Check the existing language folder for naming and formatting patterns.
- Search for an existing phrase or word before adding a duplicate.
- For language content, ask a fluent speaker, teacher, or trusted reference to review naturalness where possible.

## Adding a language

1. Copy the structure of `en/` into a new language-code folder.
2. Keep the learning intent of the English lesson, but adapt the content to the target language.
3. Add the native script, a consistent pronunciation system, and relevant grammar notes.
4. Label dialect, region, register, and cultural context whenever they affect usage.
5. Keep English as a meaning reference, not a word-for-word grammar rule.
6. Add or update links from the relevant phrase index and lesson.

Use an ISO 639-1 language code for the folder when one exists, such as `ar/`,
`fr/`, or `ja/`. If a language has important regional varieties, record that
inside the resource rather than silently presenting one variety as universal.

## Content quality

Good entries should be:

- natural for the stated language variety and situation;
- clear about literal meaning versus natural meaning;
- reusable in more than one phrase where practical;
- explicit about gender, number, politeness, or other grammar that changes the form;
- respectful of cultural differences and community preferences;
- supported by a source, native-speaker review, or reviewer note when uncertain.

Do not add machine-translated material without human review. Do not present a
regional expression, stereotype, or potentially sensitive term without context.

## File conventions

- Use Markdown files with lowercase, hyphen-separated names.
- Place lessons in `<language>/lessons/`.
- Place setting-based material in `<language>/locations/`.
- Place reusable word entries in `<language>/vocabulary/`.
- Link related lessons and vocabulary instead of duplicating large sections.
- Keep examples short enough to read aloud and practice.

## Pull requests and reviews

In a pull request, explain:

- what language, lesson, location, or vocabulary was added or changed;
- which language variety and register the examples use;
- how the content was checked or reviewed;
- any uncertain forms or follow-up work that should remain visible.

Reviewers should check both language quality and learner usefulness. A smaller,
well-reviewed contribution is preferable to a large unverified translation set.

## Maintainers and stewardship

Maintainers help keep the project accurate, welcoming, and moving forward. This
may include reviewing language content, coordinating contributors, organizing
lessons, documenting decisions, and improving the learning tools.

You do not need to be an expert in every language to help maintain the project.
People can share responsibility across language review, curriculum design,
documentation, repository care, and community support. If you would like to
take on a continuing role, open a discussion or issue describing the area you
would like to help with and the level of time you can offer.

## Questions

Open an issue or start a discussion when you are unsure about structure,
terminology, dialect labeling, or cultural context. It is better to record an
open question than to hide uncertainty in a confident-looking translation.
